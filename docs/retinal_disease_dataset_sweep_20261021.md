# Retinal Disease Dataset Sweep (2026-10-21)

## Purpose and boundary

This is a broad, source-linked inventory for expanding ActiveRetina beyond DR. It is not literally exhaustive: datasets appear, disappear, and change access terms. “Public” or “downloadable” does not automatically mean open-license, commercial-use, redistribution, or model-weight permission. Confirm the source terms at the time of acquisition and keep source-specific provenance. No gated dataset is accessed by bypassing its credential/DUA process.

The product's output must be bounded to conditions and modalities represented by valid labels. “No known target finding in the supported label set” is not equivalent to “healthy retina”; an unknown or out-of-scope image must not be silently called normal.

## Disease and label coverage found

Across the sources above, public/gated image labels cover at least: DR severity and lesions; DME; AMD (dry, wet/neovascular, drusen, geographic atrophy in some cohorts); glaucoma and glaucoma suspect; pathologic/high myopia; hypertensive retinopathy; retinal artery/vein occlusion; retinal detachment; retinal hemorrhage; toxoplasmosis scar; nevus; cataract/media opacity in some mixed cohorts; epiretinal membrane; vitreomacular traction/interface disorders; macular hole; central serous chorioretinopathy; choroidal neovascularization; retinal fluid biomarkers (IRF/SRF/PED); ROP plus/pre-plus/stage; inherited retinal dystrophies including RP and Stargardt; papilledema; and selected vascular/anatomic structures.

The MuReD v2 source record confirms 2,208 images, 20 labels, CSV train/validation lists, mixed TIFF/PNG resolutions, and CC BY 4.0. Its source components are ARIA, STARE, and RFMiD. Secondary descriptions enumerate DR, normal, media haze, optic-disc cupping, tessellation, AMD, drusen, myopia, BRVO/CRVO, optic-disc pallor/edema, CNV, retinitis/chorioretinitis, laser scars, CSR, hypertensive/arteriosclerotic retinopathy, and other. The exact v2 schema must be read from the CSV before mapping; category counts differ in older v1 summaries. Deduplicate across all component sources before any train/validation/test split.

Additional rare-disease discovery results include ocular toxoplasmosis/chorioretinitis, MS-associated retinal OCT changes, and small OCT cohorts for MacTel, central serous chorioretinopathy, macular hole, Stargardt disease, and RP. A 2023 global fundus repository review audited 127 repositories (120 then available), while an OCT review catalogs numerous disease- and task-specific collections. These are discovery baselines rather than current legal clearance. Long-tail single-disease/biomarker data widen research scope but do not make a single classifier a validated all-disease detector.

### Broader catalog pass and long-tail scope

EyeDataHub's current release is a valuable discovery index: it reports 451 source-checked records representing 446 narrowly defined dataset families across 18 categories, while explicitly warning this is not a complete census or 446 independent cohorts. It classifies no-account, authenticated, click-through, controlled, and author-contact routes and records source terms and derivation/overlap relations. I used it to widen the disease/modality search, then checked high-priority items against primary records. The catalog itself is not a dataset host, and its normalized license/access fields are screening metadata, not legal permission. Its OCT index lists 77 OCT-tagged resources, including common macular diseases, glaucoma, MS/neurologic OCT, rare macular disorders, and segmentation/biomarker data.

The open UWF disease/quality paper describes 700 images from 602 people and 689 eyes, with DR, AMD, RVO, pathologic myopia, uveitis, retinal detachment, and healthy controls, plus three-reader quality ratings. The Figshare API identifies a 9,018,811,972-byte ZIP and CC BY data license. A 15-second local transfer probe moved only about 0.5 MB (roughly 34 kB/s), so full acquisition would take about 75 hours at the observed rate; it is not included in this sprint. HYGD v1.1.0 is downloaded and checksum-verified: 747 images from 288 patients (548 GON+, 199 GON−) under ODC-By 1.0, with labels tied to a broader examination including OCT and visual fields. It is useful for a separate glaucoma head, not for defining every other source's negatives. The TOP dataset offers a strong 8-condition UWF task with patient IDs, but the primary paper states that data are available from the authors subject to Japanese export restrictions; it is a request-dependent source, not a public instant download.

OculoScope is the strongest broad fundus-label source found in the sweep: 16,530 UWF images from 8,405 patients, 38 ophthalmic diseases, and 67 fundus-feature annotations, with expert consensus labels. Access is less straightforward than the phrase “publicly available” suggests: the Figshare article API says its two downloadable archives contain only the test sets, while the full collection is separately linked and the Zenodo listing is 1.3 TB with restricted files. Its 64-MB Figshare test archive is not a training source and its transfer was stopped after a speed check. MultiEYE similarly has a public Hugging Face repository marked MIT, but currently only a 24-byte README plus a 27.26-GB ZIP; without a manifest, working loader, and label-rights/provenance inspection it is not eligible for this sprint's training. The UWF DR collection by Peng et al. is a more modest immediate candidate: the primary paper and Figshare record give 1,630 images/809 patients and three labels (normal, NPDR, PDR); the EyeDataHub profile's five-grade claim conflicts with the paper's data record. It contains repeated follow-up images and the provided split files are not documented as patient-disjoint. The 700-image UWF archive, OculoScope, MultiEYE, and Peng UWF archive are discovery leads, not data silently counted as acquired.

Long-tail conditions surfaced across the source catalog and reviews include retinal artery/vein occlusions, hypertensive retinopathy, optic-disc edema/pallor and papilledema, optic neuritis/MS-associated retinal changes, inherited dystrophies (e.g., RP/Stargardt), ROP, uveitis/retinitis, toxoplasmosis/chorioretinitis, central serous chorioretinopathy, MacTel, macular hole, epiretinal membrane/vitreomacular traction, and intraocular tumors. Many have only small, single-site, weakly labeled, gated, or noncommercial datasets. They can support separate exploratory heads or focused evaluation, not a blanket “all retinal disease” claim. Some require OCT/OCTA, FAF, angiography, visual fields, history, or genetics and cannot be comprehensively assessed from a fundus photograph alone.

This is not “every retinal disease.” Rare disorders (e.g., many inherited dystrophies, inflammatory/ infectious retinitis, tumors, trauma, and post-surgical states) are not sufficiently represented by general open image datasets. Some diseases require OCT, OCTA, FAF, angiography, visual fields, clinical history, or genetic testing; a color fundus-only model cannot claim to detect those comprehensively.

Also present: `external/DeepDRiD_v1_1`, `external/DRCR_AA_public`, and `external/MFIDDR_repo`; most have been used or inspected and are development data, not untouched external certification. The MFIDDR directory contains repository/sample material, not proof of full dataset access. DRCR AA contains grading tables but not image files. Re-audit exact images, patient IDs, rights, and splits before training.

The current local multi-task training script derives `problem = DR grade > 0 OR any MMRDR lesion`. This is not a valid all-retinal-disease target: a DR grade 0 record only establishes no DR under its grading protocol, not absence of glaucoma, AMD, ROP, or every other retinal disease. Do not use such labels as global “normal” negatives. Train separate modality-specific/multi-task heads with per-label known masks; preserve patient grouping and deduplicate across source-derived composites.

## Model and success criteria before training

“Every retinal disease” is not a single image-classification target. The viable computation target is a **modality-aware router plus scoped multi-label heads**: identify fundus/UWF/OCT/OCTA/ROP input and quality, predict only conditions represented by compatible supervised labels, and return “not assessed/outside supported scope” for the rest. A negative in one source means negative only for that source's adjudicated target; it does not mean globally healthy. Adult fundus, UWF, OCT/OCTA, pediatric ROP, and inherited-retina cohorts need separate routes or explicit modality/population conditioning and separate evaluation. Full-coverage binary “any retinal disease vs healthy” is not validly trainable from the union of disease-focused cohorts with incomplete and mismatched negative labels.

- Fundus and OCT are separate models (or explicitly modality-conditioned networks with separate validation); ROP and adult disease cohorts are not silently mixed.
- Use a multi-label objective with an independent known-label mask for every diagnosis. A source contributes loss only for labels it actually adjudicated.
- Patient-level split globally, across every source and source-derived composite; detect byte/perceptual duplicates before splitting. Never include benchmark test data in training or threshold selection.
- Primary report: per-condition sensitivity, specificity, AUROC/AUPRC, calibration, patient-level confidence intervals, subgroup/domain results, and quality/abstention. “Overall accuracy” is secondary and class-prevalence dependent.
- No numerical success promise before class counts, label semantics, overlap, licensing, and patient grouping are audited. A high pooled score across incompatible label definitions is not a clinical result.

There is no single lawful, immediately accessible dataset that covers every retinal disorder, all modalities, and representative negative cases. ## 2026-10-21 sprint decision and preflight

The discovery/acquisition phase found many source families, but only HYGD v1.1.0 was newly gathered for this sprint. It has 747 images from 288 patients (548 GON+ images, 199 GON- images); the publisher-provided SHA-256 list verified all 750 listed files. The 700-image broad UWF archive is about 9.02 GB and its observed local transfer rate implied roughly 75 hours. The 402-MB Peng UWF DR archive transferred at about 12 KB/s in a short probe, so it was not acquired. OculoScope's easily downloadable Figshare archive is test-only; its full Zenodo deposit is restricted. No restricted data were bypassed.

The 10-minute model decision is to use a shared pretrained ConvNeXt-Tiny fundus encoder with four **scoped outputs**, not one global disease/no-disease classifier:

- DR grade 0-4, supervised only by the datasets that provide DR grades.
- Ungradable-image flag, supervised only where the dataset encodes grade 5 as ungradable (DDR).
- Seven MMRDR-UWF lesion labels, supervised only on MMRDR.
- Glaucomatous optic neuropathy (GON) yes/no, supervised only on HYGD.

Each task has a masked loss; a source without that annotation contributes no negative label. OCT/OCTA and pediatric ROP are not in this fundus run and remain unsupported. The old global `problem = DR grade > 0 OR MMRDR lesion` target is invalid for all-retinal disease and must not be used.

Preflight produced 20,078 rows across DDR, DRTiD, DeepDRiD, MMRDR-UWF and HYGD. Exact SHA-256 audit found 21 duplicate rows, including eight duplicate groups across nominal splits; these are removed before training. HYGD patient grouping has zero patient overlap across its 70/15/15 train/validation/test split. DRTiD and DeepDRiD manifest patient IDs also do not cross their supplied splits. DDR has image-level pseudo-IDs and MMRDR has no verified patient identifiers, so those source results are not patient-disjoint estimates. All sources have prior project exposure; every resulting test score is a development diagnostic, not independent clinical validation.

Training and reporting protocol:

- Maximum of six GPU hours; pretrained ConvNeXt-Tiny fine-tuning, 320-pixel input, masked weighted DR cross-entropy plus separate quality, lesion and GON binary losses.
- Select checkpoint only by mean source/task validation loss. Do not tune thresholds or choose epochs on test data.
- Report per-source DR accuracy/macro recall and each binary task's AUROC, sensitivity, specificity and confusion matrix. No pooled "overall retinal accuracy" or global healthy label.
- A useful development candidate must show finite reproducible training, decreasing validation task loss, and above-chance held-out development discrimination on any task claimed; report failures and source limits too. This is not a clinical pass criterion.
- Save exact split manifest with image hashes, run configuration, training history, checkpoint hash, per-task test predictions and summary; keep image archives and row-level identifiers out of Git.
