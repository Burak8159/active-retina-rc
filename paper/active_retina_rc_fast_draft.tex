\title{ActiveRetina-RC: Risk-Controlled Retinal Screening with Selective Autonomy, Reacquisition, and Paired-View Evidence}
\author{Burak Aksu}

\begin{abstract}
Deep learning systems for diabetic retinopathy (DR) screening are usually evaluated as fixed-input classifiers: an image or image set is acquired first, and the model then predicts disease. Real screening workflows contain an earlier decision: whether the available retinal evidence is sufficient, whether another image should be acquired, or whether the case should be referred or reviewed. We present ActiveRetina-RC, a risk-controlled framework for retinal screening with three autonomous actions: CLEAR, REFER, and REACQUIRE/ABSTAIN. The framework separates three claims that are often conflated: paired-view acquisition value, autonomous diagnosis on acceptable inputs, and input-quality readiness. On public DeepDRiD paired-view data, five-fold patient-level cross-validation shows that paired views improve referable-DR AUROC from 0.887 to 0.905 in one suffix branch and from 0.848 to 0.905 in the swapped branch, while five-class quadratic weighted kappa improves from 0.614--0.613 to 0.689. Second-view acquisition rescues 75--80\% of first-view false negatives at high-sensitivity operating points, supporting the acquisition premise. However, external DRTiD testing falsifies a frozen DeepDRiD-derived autonomous candidate, demonstrating domain shift. On DDR, forced binary autonomy also fails: the best 99\%-sensitivity operating point reaches only 38.9\% specificity with 13 false negatives, and new full-data RTX fine-tuning over a 7,338-image DDR+DRTiD route still collapses to near-refer-all behavior under a zero-miss threshold. In contrast, validation-selected selective score fusion reaches zero false-negative clearances, 94.7\% specificity, and 46.3\% automatic coverage; augmented public-model fusion improves coverage to 66.8\% at zero false-negative clearances and 90.8\% specificity. A public dataset audit further identifies immediately accessible routes for diagnosis hardening and metadata robustness, while showing that the available retina-age mirror contains only adequate-quality images and cannot train a bad-image reacquisition detector. ActiveRetina-RC is therefore not reported as unrestricted autonomous diagnosis. It is a reproducible, fail-closed screening framework whose supported claim is high-confidence autonomous action with explicit reacquisition/abstention and evidence boundaries for future sealed certification.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Diabetic retinopathy is a leading cause of preventable vision loss. Screening programs benefit from timely retinal imaging, but the operational problem is not only classification. A clinical or community screening system must decide whether the images already acquired are sufficient, whether another retinal field should be captured, whether the case is safe for an automatic output, or whether it must be routed to manual review.

Most DR algorithms are evaluated after acquisition is complete. This design hides the acquisition decision. If an eye is easy, acquiring multiple fields may be wasteful. If an eye is borderline, a second view may rescue a first-view failure. If an image is ambiguous or out-of-distribution, a forced binary classifier may look accurate on average while still being unsafe for autonomous use. A screening product must therefore optimize a joint frontier: missed referable DR, specificity, automatic coverage, deferral, image burden, and reproducibility.

This paper develops ActiveRetina-RC, a risk-controlled framework for this frontier. The study began with the objective of non-myopic multi-field active acquisition on MFIDDR. Because MFIDDR access was not available during the work period, we deliberately re-scope the empirical study to public data that can be inspected and reproduced now. DeepDRiD supplies the paired-view acquisition evidence. DDR supplies a larger single-image stress test for selective autonomy. DRTiD supplies an external-domain failure and recovery analysis. The paper is therefore organized around what the evidence can actually support rather than around a desired product claim.

The central thesis is:
\begin{quote}
Risk-controlled retinal autonomy should be selective, acquisition-aware, and input-aware: automatic decisions are allowed only outside unsafe score and quality regions, while ambiguous or inadequate cases trigger reacquisition or review.
\end{quote}

\subsection{Contributions}
The contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A formal stop/acquire/abstain formulation for paired-view retinal screening, reporting risk, specificity, coverage, image burden, and deferral together.
\item A patient-disjoint DeepDRiD paired-view benchmark with suffix-mapping sensitivity, binary referable-DR and five-class endpoints, bootstrap intervals, and mechanism diagnostics.
\item A failure analysis showing that single-image forced binary autonomy does not reach useful specificity with zero missed referable clearances, even across multiple model families and new full-data RTX fine-tuning runs.
\item A strict selective autonomy mode on DDR that reaches zero false-negative clearances, 94.7\% specificity among automatic decisions, and 46.3\% coverage on the held-out test split.
\item An augmented public-model score fusion that improves automatic coverage to 66.8\% at the 90/0 gate, plus a conservative 54.4\%-coverage mode at 93.1\% specificity.
\item A fail-closed software package and public-data route: case runner, browser station, JSON API contract, audit logging, frozen policy artifacts, instant-access dataset audit, combined training manifest, and one-shot certification routines.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Deep Learning for Diabetic Retinopathy}
Large-scale DR screening systems have demonstrated strong performance on fundus photographs. Gulshan et al. validated a deep learning system for referable DR detection in EyePACS and Messidor settings \cite{gulshan2016}. Ting et al. extended validation to multiethnic screening cohorts and related eye diseases \cite{ting2017}. Later systems explored autonomous screening, image quality control, and robustness across acquisition conditions \cite{abramoff2018,bellemo2019}.

Public datasets have enabled reproducible progress. DeepDRiD provides DR grading and image-quality estimation data with paired regular fundus images \cite{deepdrid}. DDR provides a larger DR grading benchmark \cite{ddr}. DRTiD and related two-field datasets motivate studying macula-centered and optic-disc-centered views as complementary evidence \cite{drtid}. mBRSET offers portable-camera retinal images with ICDR labels and view metadata, but access requires credentialed data-use approval \cite{mbrset}.

\subsection{Selective Prediction and Risk Control}
Selective classification allows a model to abstain when automatic prediction is unsafe \cite{selective,selectivenet}. This is highly relevant to medical screening, where coverage and risk must be reported together. Conformal prediction and learn-then-test methods provide finite-sample tools for controlling error rates under explicit assumptions \cite{vovk2005,angelopoulos2021,angelopoulos2024}. In this work, we do not claim a full prospective clinical certificate. We use finite-sample binomial bounds and validation-selected thresholds to make the evidence boundary explicit.

\subsection{Active Acquisition}
Active learning and active acquisition choose which information to obtain next \cite{gal2017,settles2009}. In medical imaging, acquisition decisions have practical cost: additional fields require patient time, operator time, device stability, and storage. The acquisition action studied here is physical: acquire a second retinal field. The novelty is not a new feature-selection algorithm; it is a reproducible formulation of retinal screening as a stop/acquire/refer/abstain workflow with measured risk--coverage--cost consequences.

\section{Problem Formulation}
Let $Y\in\{0,1\}$ denote referable DR, with $Y=1$ for grade $\geq2$. Let $X_1$ be the initial retinal view and $X_2$ an optional paired view. A policy $\pi$ may output one of four actions:
\[
  a_\pi \in \{\mathrm{CLEAR},\mathrm{REFER},\mathrm{ACQUIRE},\mathrm{ABSTAIN}\}.
\]
After acquiring the second view, the final action is one of CLEAR, REFER, or ABSTAIN. CLEAR is the dangerous action for referable eyes; REFER is a conservative action for suspected disease; ABSTAIN routes the case to manual review.

For an automatic decision indicator $A_\pi=\mathbb{1}\{a_\pi\in\{\mathrm{CLEAR},\mathrm{REFER}\}\}$ and prediction $\widehat Y_\pi$, we report:
\[
R_\pi = \Pr(\widehat Y_\pi=0 \mid Y=1,A_\pi=1),
\]
the missed-referable risk among automatic decisions;
\[
S_\pi = \Pr(\widehat Y_\pi=0 \mid Y=0,A_\pi=1),
\]
the specificity among automatic decisions; and
\[
K_\pi = \Pr(A_\pi=1),
\]
the automatic coverage. For acquisition, we also report $\mathbb{E}[I_\pi]$, the mean number of retinal images, and deferral/abstention rate $D_\pi$.

The autonomy target used in the DDR experiment is stricter and operational:
\[
\mathrm{FN}_{\mathrm{CLEAR}}=0,\quad S_\pi \geq 0.90,
\]
with thresholds selected on validation and then evaluated once on test. Coverage is not hidden; it is the quantity to improve after the 90/0 safety gate is met.

\section{Datasets}
\subsection{DeepDRiD}
DeepDRiD v1.1 regular fundus images are used for paired-view acquisition experiments. The public source confirms paired macula-centered and optic-disc-centered images, but the inspected public metadata did not provide an official suffix-to-anatomy mapping. We therefore evaluate two suffix branches rather than naming a suffix as a clinical field. The primary endpoint is referable DR. The secondary endpoint is five-class DR grade.

\subsection{DDR}
DDR is used for immediate-open selective autonomy experiments. The working manifest contains 540 images in a balanced train/validation/test subset for early disease-capture experiments and a larger specificity-focused pool whose common held-out test set contains 1,435 images, including 946 referable positives and 489 nonreferable images. DDR is single-image data. It can support autonomous DR triage evidence, but not paired-view acquisition certification.

\subsection{DRTiD}
DRTiD is used as an external-domain stress test for the frozen DeepDRiD-derived autonomous candidate. The downloaded DRTiD archive contains 3,100 images forming 1,550 macula/optic-disc paired samples with 663 referable-positive IDs. Since the DRTiD test split was inspected during recovery analysis, it is not treated as an untouched prospective certificate.

\subsection{Instant-Access Public Route}
Because MFIDDR and mBRSET were not available at the time of this study, we audited immediate public routes rather than waiting for restricted access. Table~\ref{tab:instantdata} summarizes the resulting dataset roles. The audit found nine accessible Hugging Face datasets. The current executable training route combines the locally available DDR subset and DRTiD images into a patient/eye-level resplit manifest with 7,338 images, 5,788 patient/eye units, 4,049 referable images, 3,289 nonreferable images, and train/validation/test counts of 5,168/1,126/1,044. This route is used only for development and model stress testing, not as a sealed certificate.

\subsection{Retina-Age Metadata}
The retina-age-analysis mirror contains 9,857 images from 5,393 patients with patient-level train/validation/test splits, binary DR metadata, camera metadata, and anatomy indicators. It includes 698 DR-positive rows. However, the mirror reports \texttt{quality=Adequate} for every row, because inadequate-quality images were removed during dataset preparation. It is therefore useful for metadata robustness, but not sufficient for training the bad-image reacquisition detector required by a clinical product.

\subsection{Unavailable or Future Cohorts}
MFIDDR and mBRSET remain important future routes. MFIDDR is the ideal four-field active-acquisition dataset for the original non-myopic formulation. mBRSET is a plausible patient-level external cohort once credentialed PhysioNet access is available. Neither is used for unsupported claims here.

\section{Modeling and Policy Methods}
\subsection{Paired-View Representation}
For DeepDRiD, a frozen ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 encoder (\texttt{resnet50.a1\_in1k}) extracts image embeddings. For a subset of $k$ views with embeddings $h_1,\ldots,h_k$, the set representation is:
\[
\phi(\{h_i\}_{i=1}^k)=
\left[
\frac{1}{k}\sum_i h_i,\;
\max_i h_i,\;
k
\right],
\]
where the maximum is elementwise. This representation is permutation-aware through the cardinality term and otherwise invariant to view order. Logistic regression heads with training-fitted standardization and balanced class weighting are used for referable-DR and five-class grading.

\subsection{DeepDRiD Cross-Validation}
The final DeepDRiD benchmark uses five patient-disjoint outer folds. Within each fold, 20\% of non-validation patients are reserved for inner calibration and the remainder fit the model. Stop/acquire/defer policies are selected on inner calibration patients and evaluated on the held-out outer fold. Metrics are aggregated over out-of-fold predictions. Patient-cluster bootstrap intervals are computed over 1,000 resamples.

\subsection{Autonomous Selective Fusion}
The DDR autonomy layer uses existing serious model families: ResNet, DINO, ConvNeXt, feature ensembles, and MLP feature heads. Each produces a referable-risk score. Score-level candidates include individual scores, means, maxima, medians, top-two means, and rank-normalized variants. The selected policy is:
\[
s(x)=\mathrm{median}\{\mathrm{ranknorm}(s_m(x)):m\in\mathcal{M}\}.
\]
The frozen policy is:
\[
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{CLEAR}, & s(x)\leq 0.05000000000000001,\\
\mathrm{REFER}, & s(x)\geq 0.5875,\\
\mathrm{ABSTAIN}, & \mathrm{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The thresholds were selected on validation with zero false-negative clearance and a validation specificity reserve of 0.925, then applied to test without repair.

\subsection{Augmented Public-Model Fusion}
We also evaluate public retinal/fundus checkpoints as external score signals. Direct replacement with RETFound, DINOv2-fundus, or ClementP fundus classifiers does not improve the selective-autonomy baseline. The useful mode is signal augmentation: the ClementP EfficientNet B5 score is added to the existing DDR score family and validation-selected random convex mixtures are searched under the same fail-closed gate. The max-coverage augmented policy is:
\[
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{CLEAR}, & s_{\mathrm{aug}}(x)\leq 0.13006756756656757,\\
\mathrm{REFER}, & s_{\mathrm{aug}}(x)\geq 0.4625,\\
\mathrm{ABSTAIN}, & \mathrm{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\]
where $s_{\mathrm{aug}}$ is a rank-normalized weighted mixture over the EfficientNet, feature-ensemble, and MLP-family scores. A stricter conservative mode uses thresholds 0.09375 and 0.55. Learned fusion heads trained on an inner validation split are reported but not accepted because they introduce test false-negative clearances.

\subsection{Finite-Sample Risk Arithmetic}
For zero misses among $n$ automatically decided positive cases, the one-sided 95\% Clopper--Pearson upper bound is:
\[
1 - 0.05^{1/n}.
\]
To certify a 5\% upper bound with zero misses, at least 59 automatically decided positive-patient cases are required. This arithmetic is used to distinguish a promising zero-miss sample from a risk certificate.

\subsection{Clinical Software Package}
The software layer implements a fail-closed workflow. It includes a case runner, local browser station, JSON API endpoint, HTML clinician report, image hashes, operator metadata, model-version metadata, and audit log. It can analyze real uploaded retinal images, but unsafe or uncertified cases return manual-review or abstention actions rather than autonomous diagnosis.

\subsection{Full-Binary Fine-Tuning Route}
After selective-fusion experiments, we tested whether end-to-end fine-tuning could satisfy the stricter forced-binary target: 100\% image coverage, zero false-negative clearances, and at least 80\% specificity. A combined DDR+DRTiD route manifest was created with deterministic patient/eye-level splitting. Training was run on an RTX 3050 Ti GPU using EfficientNet-B0 with ordinal DR supervision, hard-boundary loss, pairwise ranking loss, class reweighting, and online hard-example pressure. The first configuration used 384-pixel inputs; the second used a faster 224-pixel negative-recovery configuration with stronger nonreferable clearance pressure. Both were stopped early because validation specificity remained near zero under the zero-miss threshold. These runs are reported because they falsify the hypothesis that simple end-to-end fine-tuning alone solves full-binary autonomy.

\section{Experiments}
\subsection{E1: Paired-View Value}
We compare initial-view and paired-view models under both suffix branches. Metrics include AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, ECE, five-class accuracy, and quadratic weighted kappa.

\subsection{E2: Policy Frontier}
We compare fixed one-view, fixed two-view, confidence triage, and two-stage acquisition policies. All policies report images, deferral, coverage, missed risk, and specificity.

\subsection{E3: Certification Arithmetic}
We evaluate whether available calibration cohorts can certify a 5\% missed-risk target using exact one-sided bounds. This is performed separately from empirical accuracy reporting.

\subsection{E4: External Domain Stress}
We run the frozen DeepDRiD-derived autonomous candidate on DRTiD. A failure is treated as evidence, not hidden. Recovery heads trained after this inspection are reported only as development evidence.

\subsection{E5: Selective Autonomy}
We evaluate whether any DDR model or score fusion can satisfy zero false-negative clearances and at least 90\% specificity among automatic decisions. We first test forced binary operation, then selective CLEAR/REFER/ABSTAIN operation.

\subsection{External DRTiD Falsifies the Frozen Paired-View Candidate}
The frozen DeepDRiD-derived autonomous candidate fails on DRTiD. It decides 739 of 1,550 IDs, including 310 positive IDs, but misses 118 decided positive IDs. The missed-risk point estimate is 38.1\%, with a 42.8\% one-sided 95\% upper bound. AUROC drops to 0.758 and average precision to 0.735. This result is important because it prevents an overclaim: a DeepDRiD-derived autonomous candidate is not externally certified.

A recovery search on DRTiD shows partial repairability. An ExtraTrees recovery candidate reaches a narrow zero-miss mode with 65 decided positive IDs, zero misses, 70.0\% decided-negative specificity, 15.5\% patient coverage, and a missed-risk upper bound below 5\%. Because DRTiD was inspected during recovery, this is development evidence, not a deployment certificate.

\subsection{Forced Binary DDR Autonomy Fails}
The DDR disease-capture experiment can autonomously refer all 120/120 referable test images with zero misses and a 2.47\% one-sided 95\% missed-risk upper bound. However, specificity is only 5.0\%, because almost all nonreferable images are referred. Disease capture alone is therefore not a useful autonomous screening product.

On the larger common DDR test set, forced binary operation remains inadequate. The best 99\%-sensitivity operating point reaches 98.6\% sensitivity, 38.9\% specificity, and 13 false negatives. A 100\%-sensitivity forced-binary threshold can eliminate false negatives, but specificity collapses. After adding the public EfficientNet signal and searching 4,026 augmented full-binary candidates, the best validation-zero-miss candidate still has 4 false negatives and only 45.8\% specificity on test. Full binary autonomy is therefore not supported by the current score family.

\subsection{Full-Data Fine-Tuning Does Not Rescue Forced Binary Autonomy}
The instant-access route was then made executable rather than conceptual. The combined DDR+DRTiD manifest contains 7,338 images, including 4,049 referable and 3,289 nonreferable images. After deterministic patient/eye-level resplitting, the train/validation/test partitions contain 5,168/1,126/1,044 images. The route was synced to an RTX 3050 Ti machine and trained with EfficientNet-B0 under two configurations. Table~\ref{tab:finetune} shows the early validation metrics. Both runs preserved zero false-negative clearance only by referring nearly every validation image. Specificity remained below 3\%, far from the 80\% or 90\% forced-binary target, so both runs were stopped by the pre-specified intervention rule. This negative result is central: full-binary autonomy is not merely missing a larger training loop; the current objective is learning the wrong product behavior.

\subsection{Selective Fusion Achieves 90/0}
Table~\ref{tab:autonomy} reports the decisive autonomy result. The original strict selective-fusion policy reaches zero false-negative clearances and 94.7\% specificity among automatic decisions on the held-out DDR test split, with 46.3\% automatic coverage. Adding the public EfficientNet fundus score expands the frontier: the max-coverage augmented policy reaches 66.8\% automatic coverage with zero false-negative clearances and 90.8\% specificity, while the conservative augmented policy reaches 54.4\% coverage with 93.1\% specificity. The accepted product mode can therefore be configured for maximum safe coverage or for a specificity margin.

The remaining autonomy gap is now concrete. In the max-coverage augmented mode, 477/1435 held-out test images abstain, including 194 referable and 283 nonreferable images. Among automatic decisions, 19 nonreferable images are referred and no referable image is cleared. Thus, the next model target is not to reduce missed disease inside the accepted selective policy; it is to recover abstained nonreferable cases and reduce false-positive referrals without moving any referable image into the CLEAR region.

\subsection{False-Negative Overlap}
At validation-selected 99\% sensitivity, no referable test image is missed by all six model families. The mean non-duplicate pairwise false-negative Jaccard overlap is 0.120. This supports fusion and hard-example learning: the remaining errors are not a single irreducible set shared by every architecture.

\section{Discussion}
The main result is not that a retinal model can classify every input autonomously. The main result is that a useful autonomous mode appears only after the task is formulated correctly. Forced binary classification either misses disease or refers nearly everyone. Selective autonomy changes the problem: automatic decisions are made only when a fused score is sufficiently safe, while uncertain cases abstain.

The achieved augmented mode meets the requested numerical gate on the DDR held-out evaluation: zero false-negative clearances and at least 90\% specificity among automatic decisions. The original strict fusion policy covers 46.3\% of cases. The augmented max-coverage mode covers 66.8\% at 90.8\% specificity, and the conservative augmented mode covers 54.4\% at 93.1\% specificity. This is a clinically plausible tradeoff for a first autonomous layer: clear some normal/mild cases, refer high-confidence suspected disease, and route the remainder to review or additional acquisition.

The DeepDRiD acquisition results explain how to improve beyond single-image selectivity. The second view rescues most first-view misses under high-sensitivity thresholds. Thus, the future product path is not merely a stronger classifier; it is a sequential policy: first image, selective risk estimate, acquire another field if unsafe, then apply the high-confidence CLEAR/REFER/ABSTAIN rule.

The newest full-data fine-tuning attempts sharpen this conclusion. They show that simply adding DRTiD, increasing local cohort size, moving training to GPU, and optimizing a harsher ordinal boundary loss does not automatically produce forced-binary clinical autonomy. Under a zero-miss validation threshold, the model still chooses the conservative solution: refer almost everything. This is not a software failure; it is evidence about the learning problem. To obtain a product-grade system, the next technical object should be a staged model with an explicit quality/reacquisition gate and a diagnosis head that operates only after the input is acceptable.

\subsection{Why This Is Stronger Than a Benchmark}
A benchmark classifier reports AUROC or accuracy. ActiveRetina-RC reports the decision frontier needed for product behavior: coverage, specificity, false-negative clearance, image burden, deferral, external failure, and finite-sample certification arithmetic. The DRTiD failure is part of the contribution because it prevents a misleading autonomous claim and identifies domain shift as a first-class obstacle.

\subsection{Clinical Interpretation}
The current autonomous mode is appropriate only as a high-confidence computational layer. It should not be described as unrestricted autonomous diagnosis. In a deployed workflow, ABSTAIN means manual review, and REACQUIRE means another image should be captured before diagnosis. The model's clinical utility depends on whether 54.4--66.8\% automatic coverage with zero observed false-negative clearance is operationally valuable in the target setting, and whether a quality/reacquisition gate can recover additional cases without introducing missed disease.

\section{Limitations}
First, the DDR autonomy result is single-image and does not certify paired-view acquisition. Second, the DeepDRiD suffix-to-anatomy mapping remains unofficial in inspected public metadata, so anatomy-specific field claims are avoided. Third, the historical DeepDRiD test partition was exposed during repair and is excluded from confirmatory claims. Fourth, DRTiD recovery consumed DRTiD as development evidence. Fifth, no prospective clinical cohort is used. Sixth, the strict zero-miss result is empirical on the held-out test split; it is not a regulatory certificate and does not replace independent validation. Seventh, the available retina-age mirror contains only adequate-quality rows, so it cannot train the missing low-quality-image reacquisition detector. Eighth, the full-binary fine-tuning attempts were intentionally stopped early after clear failure signals; they rule out the tested route, not every possible future architecture.

\section{Reproducibility and Data Availability}

\section{Conclusion}
ActiveRetina-RC reframes retinal screening as a risk-controlled selective acquisition problem rather than a forced binary classification problem. Public paired-view evidence shows that additional retinal fields can rescue first-view failures. Public DDR evidence shows that high-confidence selective fusion can meet a practical 90/0 autonomy gate on held-out evaluation: zero false-negative clearances, 90.8--93.1\% specificity among automatic decisions, and 54.4--66.8\% coverage depending on operating mode. New instant-access dataset auditing and RTX fine-tuning experiments show that forced full-coverage binary autonomy is still unsupported and that the next viable product shape is CLEAR/REFER/REACQUIRE. The resulting manuscript supports a reproducible arXiv-style paper and a serious IEEE-development path around selective autonomy, reacquisition, and adaptive paired-view evidence. It does not claim unrestricted clinical autonomy. The next scientific step is to train a true quality/reacquisition gate and certify the frozen staged policy, or a stronger paired-view successor, on a genuinely untouched external cohort.
