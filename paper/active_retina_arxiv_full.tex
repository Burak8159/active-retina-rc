\begin{abstract}
Operating-point evidence in diabetic-retinopathy (DR) screening depends on both the model and the history used to select its threshold. We conducted a retrospective audit separating a fixed-threshold raw-image evaluation, exposed-cohort score replay, and patient-grouped development analyses. On the source-designated MMRDR ultra-widefield test partition, a validation-selected frozen ResNet-50 detected 1,283/1,288 referable images (99.61\% sensitivity; image-level Wilson 95\% confidence interval, 99.09--99.83\%) and cleared 262/1,309 nonreferable images (20.02\% specificity; 95\% confidence interval, 17.94--22.27\%), with five false-negative images. A post-hoc SHA-256 audit found two positive test images byte-identical to model-fit images; excluding these rows left five misses among 1,286 positives and unchanged specificity. The partition is therefore not wholly image-disjoint, and neither analysis is independent external validation. On the model-selection subset, the same threshold yielded 100.00\% observed referable sensitivity and 20.50\% specificity; the zero-miss condition did not transfer, while specificity changed by only -0.48 percentage points. Patient-level uncertainty could not be estimated. Post-hoc test-score analysis found ROC-AUC 0.917 and 9.40\% specificity at 100\% observed sensitivity; these are descriptive, not deployable thresholds. In a separate exposed 981-eye pool, deterministic replay of a precomputed-score policy yielded 70.13\% specificity (270/385) with no observed referable or ungradable eye clearances, but the policy's ranks and threshold were derived from that cohort. A paired analysis found two negative CLEAR-to-REFER transitions and three referable-or-ungradable eye REFER-to-CLEAR transitions under patient-grouped calibration; specificity was 69.61\% (268/385; difference $-0.52$ percentage points; conditional patient-cluster bootstrap 95\% interval, $[-1.31,0.00]$), and the grouped procedure had three missed required eyes among 596 referable-or-ungradable eye records (3/596 versus 0/596; eye-level rate difference +0.50 points, conditional patient-cluster bootstrap interval 0.00--1.17). The endpoint is eye-level; patient clusters are the resampling unit, not the estimand. Source-stratified patient-cluster bootstrap intervals for the three source rates were 58.25--71.36\% (DDR), 93.75--100.00\% (DeepDRiD), and 58.27--74.80\% (DRTiD); exploratory contrasts are conditional on the selected policy and do not identify a source cause. Equal-source reweighting gave 76.68\%, an arithmetic composition sensitivity result, not model improvement. Nested procedures also did not preserve the replay's joint zero-miss/high-specificity point. Across five paired split seeds, candidate panels averaged 57.45\% and 56.31\% specificity; neither had a zero-miss run. A controlled simulation illustrates candidate search under stated assumptions but does not explain retinal outcomes. An explicit selection-history map distinguishes these nonexchangeable evidence objects; this is a bounded audit, not a new estimator, clinical certificate, or clinical-utility claim.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} diabetic retinopathy, fundus photography, specificity, patient-level validation, score fusion, selective classification, data leakage, risk control.

\section{Introduction}
Diabetic retinopathy (DR) screening is a thresholded decision problem, not merely a ranking problem. A model can rank disease well yet have an operating point that clears too few nonreferable eyes, or one that misses referable disease. This tension is consequential when investigators search for a zero-observed-miss threshold: each threshold change trades missed disease against false referrals, and repeated inspection of the evaluation cohort can make the selected trade-off appear more reliable than it is. The issue generalizes beyond retinal imaging. In multi-source medical pipelines, the reported operating point can depend on the cohort, candidate scores, threshold-selection rule, and prior analyst exposure. A faithful replay of a selected policy therefore answers a different question from patient-grouped development estimation or evaluation on an independent cohort.

This audit asks three questions. \textbf{RQ1:} Does a threshold chosen on a development partition retain its observed miss--specificity behavior on the dataset's source-designated test partition, and what does exact-image overlap imply for that comparison? \textbf{RQ2:} How do a same-cohort score-policy replay and patient-grouped or nested estimates differ on a repeatedly inspected multi-source cohort? \textbf{RQ3:} Does the selected specificity profile remain stable when the candidate score panel changes across paired patient-group split seeds? A controlled synthetic experiment separately tests whether candidate search alone can create a specificity--zero-miss transfer trade-off under explicit assumptions; it is not used to explain the retinal results. The questions are descriptive and retrospective; they were not prospectively registered, and the empirical analyses do not support population-level hypothesis tests.

The evidence objects are deliberately kept separate. The MMRDR-UWF ResNet-50 addresses RQ1 and is an image-level evaluation on the dataset's source-designated test partition; two exact-byte test duplicates were also present in model-fit data, so it is not wholly image-disjoint. The 70.13\% result addresses deterministic replay of precomputed scores on the exposed DDR/DeepDRiD/DRTiD cohort. Grouped and nested procedures on that same cohort address internal development sensitivity, not independent confirmation. Candidate-panel comparisons address RQ3 across five paired split seeds. The separate 84.16\% multi-source ResNet-50 threshold-crossfit diagnostic and 47.15\% ConvNeXt-Tiny validation result are secondary, post-selection development evidence. Their cohorts, artifacts, endpoints, and selection histories differ; these percentages are not head-to-head comparisons. No independent external or prospective clinical validation is reported.

We frame the work as a reproducibility and validation-methods case study, not a new diagnostic algorithm. Nested cross-validation and selection-bias analysis are established tools \cite{cawley2010,varma2006}; recent DR studies also compare decision strategies at fixed sensitivity and evaluate referable-risk transfer across datasets \cite{jena2026,sheng2026orderdr}. We therefore do not claim those questions as new in isolation. Our contribution is empirical and bounded to one historically exposed project: showing how operating-point conclusions vary across replay, patient-grouped procedures, candidate panels, and a separate fixed-threshold within-dataset image test. A controlled simulation isolates one candidate-search mechanism under stated Gaussian and exchangeability assumptions, with a perfectly correlated negative control; it demonstrates possibility under that model, not causation in the retinal data. The five-seed panel contrast is small on average, changes direction across seeds, and neither panel reaches zero misses. We make no claim that this case study introduces a new estimator or risk-control theorem.

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item We report a frozen ResNet-50's validation-to-official-test operating-point transfer on MMRDR-UWF, including image-level errors, the low specificity at the selected threshold, and the missing patient identifiers that prevent cluster-aware uncertainty estimation.
\item We fully describe the source-conditional score-policy replay on the 981-case DDR/DeepDRiD/DRTiD pool and contrast it with fixed, nested, and post-hoc patient-grouped procedures, while separating gradable disease labels from ungradable cases.
\item We quantify candidate-panel sensitivity across five paired patient-group split seeds and show the per-source and pooled outcomes, including the fact that neither panel achieved zero misses in any run.
\item We report a reproducible synthetic mechanism experiment with an identical-channel negative control, separating candidate-search behavior from retinal-cohort evidence.
\item We reconcile these distinct evidence objects in a claim-to-evidence map, including secondary post-selection image-model diagnostics and the corrected validation-manifest overlap, so replay, internal development, and held-out within-dataset testing are not conflated with independent external validation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{DR screening and operating points.} Large DR-screening studies have evaluated deep-learning systems on retinal photographs in multiethnic, primary-care, and lower-resource settings \cite{gulshan2016,ting2017,abramoff2018,bellemo2019}. Their clinical interpretation depends on the task definition, reference standard, operating threshold, and study population, not only discrimination. Public datasets such as DDR and DeepDRiD support work on grading and image quality, while multi-source datasets introduce differences in acquisition and label construction \cite{ddr,deepdrid,drtidpaper}. The present work does not re-evaluate those systems; it audits how threshold provenance and candidate choice shape conclusions in one development pipeline.

A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis of regulator-approved fundus-based systems synthesized 82 studies, 887,244 examinations, 25 devices, and 28 countries. Its pooled sensitivity/specificity estimates were 0.93/0.90 per patient and 0.92/0.93 per eye; meta-regression identified disease threshold, national-income level, image gradability, pupil dilation, reference standard, and diagnostic criteria as factors explaining between-study heterogeneity \cite{wang2026approveddr}. These estimates concern a distinct evidence base and are not a performance comparator for ActiveRetina. They motivate reporting an operating point together with its cohort, acquisition conditions, endpoint, and selection history; this case study neither estimates real-world performance nor explains the review's heterogeneity.

\paragraph{Decision strategies and external threshold transfer in DR.} On 5,270 EyePACS images (1,366 labeled as DR), Jena and Rayguru compare five AI decision strategies at 95\% and 90\% sensitivity targets; their best single-model strategy has 17.5\% specificity at 95\% sensitivity, while a weighted ensemble at 90\% sensitivity reduces unnecessary referrals by nearly 25\% relative to the single-model 95\% setting \cite{jena2026}. This establishes that comparing DR decision strategies at fixed sensitivity, and documenting the sensitivity--specificity trade-off, are not novel contributions here. We neither rerun their strategies nor make a head-to-head performance claim. Our narrower question is how operating-point evidence in a historically reused retinal pipeline changes across score replay, grouped and nested development procedures, candidate-panel sensitivity, and a separate frozen image-model test. Sheng et al. report external validation of severity grading and referable-risk stratification with ORDER-DR \cite{sheng2026orderdr}, illustrating why threshold selection and external testing must be distinguished. Our MMRDR result is a frozen within-dataset held-out image test, not external transfer; the separate score-policy replay is exposed and cannot estimate transfer to new cases. EyePACS and MMRDR differ in cohort, labels, inputs, models, and estimands, so these figures are contextual prior art and must not be interpreted as a performance comparison.

Three recent studies further bound the interpretation of UWF results. Li et al. evaluated ophthalmic foundation models across Danish and Greenlandic UWF populations; external discrimination was lower than within-population performance, and calibration remained poor even after sequential adaptation \cite{li2026uwf}. A separate medRxiv preprint compared generalist and ophthalmic foundation models on BRSET and mBRSET, likewise reporting external discrimination loss and residual calibration concerns \cite{li2026mbrset}. Poyrazer et al. compared frozen MedSigLIP, RETFound, and EfficientNet-B0 encoders with a shared trained head on APTOS and zero-tuning MESSIDOR-2; similar development AUCs separated under external testing, and development temperature scaling did not resolve external calibration error \cite{poyrazer2026frozen}. Their study directly addresses frozen-representation transfer and calibration, so we make no claim to introduce either question. Our MMRDR classifier instead uses a frozen ImageNet ResNet-50 and a study-trained six-logit head; this manuscript's distinct contribution is an audit of operating-point provenance and selection history alongside its unfavorable, fixed-threshold within-dataset test. The cited studies use different cohorts, endpoints, models, and estimands and are not performance comparators. Collectively, they make it especially important not to infer clinical transfer from our within-dataset result or from score ranking alone.

\paragraph{Selection, dependence, and validation.} Reusing finite evaluation data for model selection can bias reported performance, and nested validation is designed to separate selection from assessment within a specified development procedure \cite{cawley2010,varma2006}. When observations share patients or other clusters, folds should preserve that grouping to avoid leakage across training and evaluation \cite{roberts2017}. However, grouping within a later analysis does not undo earlier cohort-wide inspection, global candidate generation, or checkpoint selection. We therefore report grouped results as internal development diagnostics where that history applies, rather than treating the fold construction alone as proof of independence.

Cutoff optimism and reuse of medical-imaging test data are not new problems. Leeflang et al. quantified bias in sensitivity and specificity after data-driven cutoff selection and examined alternative estimation approaches; Westphal and Zapf addressed inference when multiple diagnostic tests or cutpoints are compared \cite{leeflang2008,westphal2024}. Yamanaka et al. studied repeated reuse of a test set during post-market selection and integration of bone-scintigraphy models, reporting performance bias and evaluating a differential-privacy-based mitigation \cite{yamanaka2026}. These works preclude claiming that cutoff-selection bias or test reuse is itself novel. We do not implement their corrections. The present contribution is narrower: a retrospective DR case study that keeps exposed score replay, patient-grouped development, candidate-panel sensitivity, and fixed-threshold within-dataset image testing separate in the claim-to-evidence account.

\paragraph{Selective prediction and risk control.} Selective classification studies the accuracy--coverage trade-off when a model may abstain or defer uncertain cases \cite{geifman2017,selectivenet}. Conformal prediction and learn-then-test procedures provide formal risk-control tools under their assumptions and prespecified procedures \cite{vovk2005,angelopoulos2021,angelopoulos2024}. An empirical threshold that yields no observed misses on a reused cohort is not equivalent to such a guarantee. This paper does not implement a formal risk-control method; it makes the distinction explicit because several development analyses report a zero-observed-miss point.

\paragraph{Reporting guidance and contribution boundary.} TRIPOD+AI and CLAIM, including its 2024 update, emphasize transparent reporting of participants or cases, data partitions, model evaluation, and limitations \cite{tripodai,claim,claim2024}. These reporting guidelines do not themselves establish validity. Our contribution is not a new DR architecture, a new cross-validation estimator, or a clinical validation study. It is a bounded empirical audit that brings replay fidelity, selection history, grouped development estimates, candidate-panel sensitivity, and one fixed-threshold within-dataset image test into a single claim-to-evidence account.

\section{Study Question and Endpoints}
For eye/image record $i$, let $Y_i\in\{0,1\}$ denote gradable referable DR, with $Y_i=1$ corresponding to referable disease (dataset grades 2--4 under the harmonized project definition), and let $U_i=1$ mark an ungradable eye. The policy emits $A_i\in\{\text{CLEAR},\text{REFER}\}$. We report eye-level specificity among gradable nonreferable eyes,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Sp}=\frac{\sum_i\mathbb{1}(Y_i=0,U_i=0,A_i=\text{CLEAR})}{\sum_i\mathbb{1}(Y_i=0,U_i=0)},
\end{equation}
the missed-referable-eye count $M=\sum_i\mathbb{1}(Y_i=1,U_i=0,A_i=\text{CLEAR})$, missed-ungradable-eye count $M_U=\sum_i\mathbb{1}(U_i=1,A_i=\text{CLEAR})$, and action assignment $C=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i\mathbb{1}(A_i\in\{\text{CLEAR},\text{REFER}\})$. These outcomes are image/eye-level, not patient-level. Here $C=100\%$ means every record receives one of the two computational actions; it does not imply independent generalization, image-quality adequacy, or clinical usability.

The prespecified development target used in the project was a useful specificity at zero observed misses and full binary action assignment. This paper does not present that target as a validated safety guarantee. We use ``zero-miss'' only as a finite-sample descriptive observation unless explicitly qualified otherwise.

The principal raw-image outcome is the fixed-threshold primary operating point on the official test: false-negative count and sensitivity are reported first, with specificity and referral burden as the paired trade-off. Overall accuracy is not the primary endpoint because it depends on case mix and can obscure the miss/referral balance. The 70.13\% value is a secondary deterministic score-replay statistic, not model accuracy. The 84.16\% value is a separate post-selection development diagnostic and is not used to support the primary claim. A 100\% binary action-assignment rate means the evaluator emitted an action for every supplied row; it is not clinical coverage or proof every case was correctly resolved.

Dataset availability and use remain subject to the corresponding licenses and access conditions. We do not redistribute raw images.

\subsection{Exposure and split history}
The score references and operating thresholds in the frozen policy were derived from the same repeatedly inspected 981-case cohort. Thus, the replay answers ``can the recorded recipe reproduce its recorded result?'' It does not answer ``what performance should be expected on new patients?'' Patient-grouped calibration folds below prevent fold-specific calibration from using the held-out patients, but cannot erase prior inspection of the cohort or prior global model-family exploration. We label all such results development diagnostics.

\section{Frozen Policy}
For source $d$, each component score $z_{idm}$ is transformed into a percentile midrank $r_{idm}$ against the stored, sorted source-specific reference vector of size $n_d$:
\begin{equation}
r_{idm}=\frac{L(z_{idm})+R(z_{idm})+1}{2n_d},
\end{equation}
where $L$ and $R$ count reference values strictly less than and less than-or-equal to the score. The rank-fused risk score is a source-specific convex combination:
\begin{align}
s_{\mathrm{DDR}}&=0.50r_{\mathrm{APTOS}}+0.10r_{\mathrm{ordinal}}+0.40r_{\mathrm{audit}},\\
s_{\mathrm{DeepDRiD}}&=0.20r_{\mathrm{ordinal-quality}}+0.40r_{\mathrm{source-balanced}}+0.40r_{\mathrm{fundus}},\\
s_{\mathrm{DRTiD}}&=0.72r_{\mathrm{rank-pair}}+0.08r_{\mathrm{fundus-margin}}+0.20r_{\mathrm{geometry}}.
\end{align}
An eye is referred when its score reaches its source-specific cut point; the DDR rule also refers cases whose quality score reaches its stored override threshold. The cut points are 0.22224 for DDR, 0.435849 for DeepDRiD, and 0.34496 for DRTiD; the DDR quality override is 0.0018599. These numerical cut points and rank-reference vectors come from the exposed cohort. They are published here to make the replay auditable, not as proposed portable clinical thresholds.

The implementation is a deterministic score-policy evaluator. It requires three precomputed component outputs per source and a source identifier. It is not a single end-to-end image model. Exact source checkpoints and preprocessing implementations are not all present in the current repository state; accordingly, the replay should not be described as complete raw-image inference. Throughout, ``deterministic replay'' means recomputing actions from supplied component-score rows and a frozen policy; ``internal development estimation'' means grouped or nested analysis on the previously inspected project cohorts; and ``independent external validation'' means evaluation on a new cohort untouched by training, calibration, threshold/policy selection, or prior project decisions. No independent external validation is reported here.

\section{Evaluation Procedures}
\subsection{Frozen same-cohort replay}
We reran the frozen policy on each of the 981 stored component-score rows and compared every emitted action and risk score with the recorded replay. We then reconstructed source-level confusion counts and checked the headline grouped/nested counts against their saved summary artifacts. The executable command, required file inventory, environment dependency, checksums, and scope are specified in the accompanying reproduction-package README. This validates deterministic replay of supplied scores and manuscript transcription; it does not regenerate image features, retrain component models, or provide independent external validation.

\subsection{Patient-grouped calibration and nested selection}
The fixed-recipe analysis used five patient-grouped folds. For each fold, source-specific calibration was performed without its held-out patients, and predictions were aggregated over the held-out folds. The component model family and the cohort had already been inspected globally; this is not an independent confirmatory evaluation. For a paired descriptive comparison, held-out actions were joined to replay actions by source and eye and checked against the patient manifest and labels. Specificity differences were bootstrapped by resampling negative patient clusters within each source, retaining paired decisions and each resampled eye denominator (50,000 replicates; seed 20261020). For the required-eye miss-rate difference, we separately resampled the 574 source--patient clusters containing referable or ungradable eyes within source, retaining each selected cluster's paired replay and cross-fit actions and eye count, and recomputed the pooled eye-level rate difference over referable-or-ungradable eye records (50,000 replicates; seed 20261020). The estimand remains eye-level; patient-cluster resampling changes the resampling unit, not the endpoint. Both percentile intervals condition on fixed decisions and do not include prior model, candidate, policy, or threshold-selection uncertainty; neither is confirmatory.

The nested analysis used five outer patient-grouped folds and four inner folds. Blend weights were searched on a simplex grid with 0.1 increments; the risk threshold was the minimum required-positive score in the training portion. The inner search compared weight/margin combinations, prioritizing zero inner misses before specificity; tested margins were 0, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.03, 0.05, 0.075, 0.1, 0.15, and 0.2. The selected combination was evaluated on its outer fold. This protects each outer fold from that run's direct fitting, but the data and component family remain previously exposed. Separately, a post-hoc margin sweep searched 13 values from 0 to 0.2 and chose source-specific margins after inspecting held-out predictions; that frontier is included solely to show the attainable retrospective trade-off and is not an unbiased candidate.

For panel $p$ and paired split seed $s$, let $\widehat{\mathrm{Sp}}_{p,s}=\mathrm{TN}_{p,s}/N_-,$ where $N_-=385$ gradable nonreferable eyes. We summarize the paired contrast $\Delta_s=100(\widehat{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\mathrm{all},s}-\widehat{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\mathrm{shortlist},s})$ in percentage points, together with its arithmetic mean, observed range, sign count, and the miss count for each panel. This response profile makes split sensitivity visible instead of treating one favorable partition as the panel effect. Since the seeds reuse patients and the artifact universe is exposed, these summaries are descriptive and are not confidence intervals, hypothesis tests, or estimates of performance on new patients.

\subsection{Candidate-set sensitivity protocol}
We repeated the same five-outer/four-inner selector under two panels using fixed integer RNG seeds 20261015--20261019. The first value identifies the original split; four additional values were then run to assess split sensitivity. These date-shaped integers are RNG identifiers, not calendar dates, and are not five independent replications. The label-screened panel contains the frozen recipe and channels previously identified by a full-cohort oracle as adding at least one clearance; this screening used the full cohort and is explicitly label-informed. The expanded panel contains that recipe plus every complete, finite, nonconstant numeric score column aligned to each source in the stored CSV artifact library. Identifiers, labels, quality, and action fields are excluded; exact duplicate vectors are collapsed; rank transforms are fit within each training fold. Panel sizes, including the recipe, are 11/1/7 and 44/40/119 columns for DDR/DeepDRiD/DRTiD. Ungradable cases are forced to REFER and excluded from gradable specificity; the reported miss counts are gradable referable cases assigned CLEAR. The contrast changes both panel size and the prior screening history, so it does not isolate a causal effect of dimensionality or label screening. We summarize seed-to-seed ranges descriptively, not as confidence intervals: folds overlap in training data and the same patients are reused across all five splits. The historical artifact library and model families were created after repeated inspection of this cohort, so this does not estimate total search optimism or external performance.

\subsection{Controlled zero-miss candidate-search simulation}
We generated equally informative Gaussian score channels with positive scores distributed as $\mathcal{N}(1.5,1)$ and negative scores as $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$. Within-case channel correlation was set to $\rho\in\{0,0.5,0.9,1\}$ using a shared standard-normal component plus channel-specific noise. Each channel's threshold was the minimum of 60 development-positive scores, giving zero development misses by construction. Among $K\in\{1,5,20,100\}$ channels, we selected the one with the greatest specificity on 200 development-negative observations and evaluated the selected threshold on independent synthetic samples of 300 positives and 500 negatives. We ran 2,000 replicates per condition with fixed seed 20261016; ties selected the first channel. A perfect-correlation condition ($\rho=1$) is a negative control because all candidate channels are identical.

We separately varied the development-negative selection sample size over 50, 200, and 1,000 cases for the 100-candidate panel. For each replicate and correlation, the development-positive and independent test samples were shared across all three sizes, while the development-negative samples were nested prefixes of a common 1,000-case sample. Thus, differences across sample sizes isolate how many synthetic negatives inform candidate ranking; these sensitivity replicates use separate, fixed random-number streams and are distinct from the primary simulation. The analysis remains synthetic and does not posit a retinal score distribution.

For one prespecified channel with iid continuous positive scores and no ties, exchangeability gives $\Pr(\text{zero test misses})=n_d/(n_d+n_t)$ when the threshold is the minimum among $n_d$ development-positive scores and $n_t$ independent test positives. Under an idealized panel of $K$ independent, identically distributed channels selected by greatest \emph{true} specificity, the corresponding probability is $K\,\mathrm{B}(1+n_t/n_d,K)$, where $\mathrm{B}$ is the beta function. To see this, transform positive scores through their common CDF so they are uniform on $[0,1]$. For each channel let $U_k$ be the minimum of its $n_d$ development scores; selection by the largest true-specificity threshold chooses $U_{(K)}=\max_k U_k$. Conditional on this threshold, the probability all $n_t$ test-positive scores exceed it is $(1-U_{(K)})^{n_t}$. The variable $V=1-U_{(K)}$ has density $K n_d v^{n_d-1}(1-v^{n_d})^{K-1}$ on $0<v<1$, giving $\mathbb{E}[V^{n_t}]=K n_d\int_0^1v^{n_d+n_t-1}(1-v^{n_d})^{K-1}dv=K\,\mathrm{B}(1+n_t/n_d,K)$. For $n_d=60$ and $n_t=300$, this idealized reference is 16.67\%, 0.397\%, 0.00188\%, and $1.04\times10^{-6}\%$ for $K=1,5,20,100$, respectively. These order-statistic references assume the true channel specificity is known; the simulation instead selects by finite-sample estimated specificity and allows correlated channels, so its results need not equal the formula. Neither result is a clinical risk guarantee.

\subsection{Clean-development candidate transfer diagnostic}
A ResNet-50 initialized from a public MMRDR-UWF model was fine-tuned at 448-pixel input on original training partitions after excluding all patient groups mapped to the 981 reference cases. Its patient-disjoint development split selected the checkpoint and two thresholds. The frozen candidate was then evaluated on the repeatedly inspected reference cohort. We also computed an offline union action, CLEAR if either the old baseline or candidate cleared. Because the reference cohort has extensive prior project exposure, this is a transfer diagnostic, not an independent test or a new baseline.

\subsection{Frozen MMRDR-UWF classifier and official-test protocol}
One implementation detail affects interpretation of the classifier score: the six-logit head has outputs for grades 0--5, while this experiment contains categorical labels only for grades 0--4, and the referable score aggregates logits 2--5. The grade-5 logit therefore has no direct grade-5 categorical examples. It may still receive indirect gradients from binary referable, pairwise-ranking, and hard-boundary losses, all of which act on the aggregate referable score. Because the checkpoint is unavailable, its contribution cannot be isolated; we do not infer the direction or magnitude of any effect on the reported test metrics.
The raw-image classifier uses the \texttt{resnet50.a1\_in1k} ImageNet-1K encoder with 224-by-224 RGB inputs. Images are bicubically resized, center-cropped to 95\% of the scaled image, and normalized using ImageNet channel statistics. The encoder is frozen; cached 2,048-dimensional features feed a trained six-logit linear head (12,294 trainable parameters including bias). The six logits represent grades 0--5; MMRDR-UWF labels in this experiment occupy grades 0--4, with referable disease defined as grade $\geq2$. The referable-risk score is the log-sum-exp of grade-2--5 logits minus the log-sum-exp of grade-0--1 logits. This is a study-trained classifier using a pretrained frozen backbone, not a fully fine-tuned retinal foundation model.

The official UWF training partition supplies training rows; 1,172 images were carved out for image-level development and threshold selection because patient identifiers were unavailable in the export. The selected checkpoint is epoch 20. At its frozen validation threshold (the minimum validation referable score, $-1.8022103309631348$ on the uncalibrated logit scale), the classifier detected all 572/572 referable images and cleared 123/600 nonreferable images. The training implementation combined inverse-frequency weighted ordinal cross-entropy, binary referable-risk cross-entropy, pairwise ranking loss, and a hard-boundary loss. The checkpoint does not preserve all optimizer and loss hyperparameters or the feature-cache hash; exact independent retraining is consequently not reproducible from the public checkout.

The primary fixed-threshold analysis used the source-designated official test partition after checkpoint and threshold freeze, with no test-driven model, threshold, or preprocessing changes. After this primary result had been inspected, we computed post-hoc score-ranking and sensitivity-constrained frontier diagnostics from the test export; these did not alter the model or operating point and are descriptive only. The test export contains no patient identifiers. The dataset descriptor states that UWF was partitioned at the patient level to prevent leakage \cite{mmrdr}; we cannot independently audit patient separation from these rows or compute patient-clustered uncertainty. In addition, a SHA-256 audit of original image bytes across the 10,404-row local UWF manifest found eight exact-duplicate groups: six within one official partition and two crossing official training/test. Both copies in the latter training rows fell in the internal model-fit split; neither group crossed the internal fit/model-selection boundary. Both test copies were referable and referred. The source-designated test therefore is not wholly image-disjoint from model fitting. The test was already inspected, and the audit cannot detect transformed near-duplicates or other patient overlap. We report image-level metrics and describe this as a within-dataset test-partition evaluation with documented exact-image overlap, not independent-site or prospective clinical evaluation.

After the frozen result had been inspected, a secondary score diagnostic was computed from the hash-verified prediction export. ROC-AUC uses the Mann--Whitney statistic with average ranks for ties; average precision groups tied scores before updating precision and recall. For each stated minimum-sensitivity constraint, unique observed test scores were enumerated and the point with greatest test specificity satisfying the constraint was retained. This test-label-derived envelope is post-hoc, is not model selection, and must not be used as a deployment threshold. The script emits aggregate-only JSON. Patient identifiers are absent, so no patient-clustered uncertainty is available for these diagnostics.

\subsection{MMRDR-UWF local development and multi-source fine-tuning stress tests}
The separate MMRDR-UWF local development subset contains 1,172 images (572 referable and 600 nonreferable at the grade-$\geq2$ boundary) from the multimodal MMRDR release \cite{mmrdr}. Patient identifiers were not available in the exported rows, so the local split is image-level; the official test partition was not evaluated. Candidate and threshold comparisons on this subset are therefore development-only. A later four-source fine-tuning run used DDR, DeepDRiD, DRTiD, and IDRiD training images \cite{idrid}, with a 1,337-image-row manifest spanning 982 eyes. A subsequent audit found that all 981 historical reference eyes appeared in its validation rows. This correction supersedes an earlier claim that the reference eyes were held apart; the fine-tuning experiment is exposed relative to the historical reference and cannot provide independent validation.

\subsection{Multi-source ResNet-50 threshold-crossfit diagnostic}
The separate multi-source candidate uses a ResNet-50 score export and a manifest of 6,348 training images and 1,338 validation images from DDR, DeepDRiD, and DRTiD. The manifest has 929 validation patient groups and no patient overlap between its train and validation partitions. The stored checkpoint epoch (epoch 6) was selected using the complete validation split under the run's zero-miss operating-point criterion. We therefore freeze that checkpoint and cross-fit only the decision threshold as a post-selection sensitivity analysis; the procedure cannot undo the earlier full-validation checkpoint selection.

Patients are sorted by descending validation-image count, with patient ID as the deterministic tie-breaker, then assigned whole to the currently smallest of five folds (fold-index tie-breaker). For each fold, a scalar threshold maximizing image-level accuracy is selected from the other four folds' scores and applied to the held-out fold. This yields group-disjoint threshold fitting and scoring, but it is not nested model selection, an unbiased estimate of the training pipeline, or external validation. The binary action rule is REFER when score is greater than or equal to threshold, otherwise CLEAR; no abstention state is present in this diagnostic.

\subsection{End-to-end image-model development run}
We also audited the saved source-balanced ConvNeXt-Tiny run as a distinct experiment. It uses the pretrained \texttt{convnext\_tiny.fb\_in22k\_ft\_in1k} encoder with eye-level image bags (one DDR image or paired DeepDRiD/DRTiD views), plus grade, risk, and quality heads. The saved configuration uses log-mean-exp view-risk pooling at temperature 0.25 and validation-selected ordinal-risk/quality thresholds. Its manifest contains 4,675 training eyes, 982 validation eyes, and 1,236 test eyes; test evaluation is explicitly marked skipped. The saved run completed one source-balanced epoch of 1,200 batches. The validation cohort and thresholds have been repeatedly inspected. The checkpoint and eye-level predictions are excluded from the public checkout, so this manuscript does not claim clean-clone reproduction of the model result.

\subsection{Statistics}
Specificity is reported as an empirical ratio with its numerator and denominator. We do not attach a population confidence interval to the headline result because threshold and reference selection reuse the cohort and patient dependence cannot be ignored. A standard exact binomial interval describes sampling for a fixed procedure but does not correct post-selection bias in this setting \cite{clopperpearson,cawley2010}. The 75\% specificity target corresponds arithmetically to at least $\lceil0.75\times385\rceil=289$ true negatives on this denominator; it is a development arithmetic target, not a clinical threshold.

\subsection{Selection-history evidence map}
For each analysis object, we recorded four fields: (1) input and unit (raw image, precomputed score row, or generated observation); (2) outcome-dependent choices, including fitting, checkpoint/candidate selection, score normalization, and threshold selection; (3) partition and identity evidence, including whether patient grouping was observed, verified, or unavailable; and (4) the narrowest inference supported by that history. Evidence class was assigned from this record, not from the metric value or the label ``test.'' Exact replay of the same records supports implementation consistency only. Fold-held estimates remain internal development evidence when the cohort or candidate family was globally exposed before folding. A frozen model evaluated on a dataset-designated split is a within-dataset evaluation; image overlap or missing patient identifiers further restricts its independence. A threshold envelope selected from inspected test labels is post-hoc descriptive evidence. Independent external validation requires a cohort unused for training, selection, calibration, repair, or prior project decisions. Unrecovered patient linkage or selection history is marked unavailable rather than presumed independent. This map organizes the evidence; it does not adjust outcomes or provide a risk guarantee.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Frozen raw-image classifier: official MMRDR-UWF test}
On the development partition, the frozen validation threshold detected 572/572 referable images and cleared 123/600 nonreferable images (20.50\% specificity). The one-sided exact 95\% upper bound for the image-row miss fraction is 0.81\%; it is not a patient-level risk bound because patient identifiers are absent and the calculation treats image rows as independent. Every test image received a binary CLEAR or REFER action (100\% action assignment), which is an implementation property, not evidence that every case was safely resolved.

All five false-negative clearances were grade 2, the lowest referable category. \subsection{The replay is exact, but it is in-sample}
The deterministic replay reproduced the frozen counts: 270 true negatives among 385 gradable nonreferable cases (70.13\%), no observed referable clearances among 463 gradable referable cases, no ungradable case cleared, and 100\% binary action assignment. Table~\ref{tab:cohort} reports the source-specific numerators and denominators: specificity was 134/206 (65.05\%) for DDR, 51/52 (98.08\%) for DeepDRiD, and 85/127 (66.93\%) for DRTiD. Source-stratified patient-cluster intervals and exploratory pairwise contrasts are reported above. Reweighting these same three rates to one third per source gives 76.68\% (patient-cluster bootstrap 95\% percentile interval, 72.93--80.32\%), compared with 70.13\% under the observed negative-eye mixture. This 6.55-point difference is arithmetic standardization of the same exposed outcomes, not a model improvement, representative target-population estimate, or external validation; Fig.~\ref{fig:source} shows both summaries. The score reference vectors and thresholds were estimated on the same cases, so the 70.13\% point estimate is an in-sample development replay, not an estimate of future-patient performance.

\subsection{Accuracy-threshold diagnostic on the multi-source ResNet-50}
The threshold-only grouped cross-fit produced 1,126 correct classifications among 1,338 validation images (84.16\% accuracy): 660 true positives, 466 true negatives, 99 false positives, and 113 false negatives. Sensitivity was 85.38\%, specificity 82.48\%, and binary action assignment 100\%. The five fold thresholds were 1.266855, 1.266855, 1.220113, 1.266855, and 0.809623. Fold-level aggregate counts and thresholds are provided in the machine-readable supplement. This accuracy is descriptive only: it misses 113 of 773 referable images, and the checkpoint epoch was selected with all validation labels before threshold cross-fitting. The result is therefore post-selection development evidence, not an unbiased validation estimate, an external test, or evidence of autonomous safety. It is not used to claim that a clinical performance target was met.

\subsection{End-to-end model result is a separate, lower development point}

\subsection{Synthetic mechanism: candidate search can trade specificity for zero-miss transfer}
The controlled simulation demonstrates the proposed mechanism only within its specified data-generating process. Figure~\ref{fig:arxiv-panel-simulation} and Table~\ref{tab:arxiv-panel-simulation} summarize the operating points. With independent channels ($\rho=0$), increasing the searched panel from one to 100 raised mean selected-development specificity from 22.51\% to 55.45\% and mean independent-test specificity from 22.48\% to 52.89\%; zero-miss frequency over 300 test positives fell from 18.10\% (Wilson Monte Carlo interval, 16.47--19.85\%) to 0/2,000 (0--0.19\%). \subsection{The later candidate does not provide a safe lift}
The clean-development-trained ResNet-50 candidate alone cleared 54/385 nonreferable cases (14.03\%) and missed two referable cases. OR-combining candidate CLEAR actions with the frozen baseline increased true negatives from 270 to 273 (70.91\%, a 0.78-point descriptive increase) but still missed two referable cases. The 75\% arithmetic target would require 289 true negatives; the union remains 16 short and violates the zero-miss condition. The baseline remains frozen and unchanged.

\subsection{The apparent gap to 75\% is not a license to tune on labels}
On the frozen denominator, 75\% requires 19 additional true negatives beyond the original 270. Later exposed-cohort experiments reduce this arithmetic gap but either introduce misses or use thresholds selected after inspecting reference labels. No result in this study establishes a 75\% zero-miss candidate on new cases.

\subsection{A second domain stress test does not remove the evidence gap}
On the MMRDR-UWF validation subset, the best tested RETFound feature head at a within-validation zero-miss operating point cleared 63/600 negatives (10.50\%) with AUROC 0.919; an RBF-SVM cleared 60/600 (10.00\%). The internal ResNet+DINO/448-pixel ResNet reference cleared 202/600 (33.67\%; AUROC 0.932). Adding 25\% of the RETFound-logistic rank produced 207/600 (34.50\%; AUROC 0.940), five additional clearances. The representation was derived from the retinal foundation model RETFound \cite{retfound}. Since the same validation labels informed both candidate and threshold comparison, these are internal development estimates, not a held-out model comparison.

On the audited 982-eye fine-tuning validation manifest, the MMRDR-initialized warm start reached 95/386 gradable negatives at zero observed misses (24.61\%). Fine-tuning at epochs 1, 2, and 3 yielded 63/386 (16.32\%), 74/386 (19.17\%), and 73/386 (18.91\%), respectively. The warm start remained the strongest tested point, but every historical reference eye was in the validation rows. These figures are reported to preserve the provenance correction and training outcome; they do not establish an independent gain or a clean epoch-selection comparison.

\section{Discussion}
The key finding is methodological: a zero-miss specificity point can look stable in replay and then lose zero-miss status when calibration is moved into patient-grouped folds. The observed 70.13\% is therefore kept as a historical frozen reference, not promoted to an independent performance claim. Patient grouping is necessary to prevent same-patient leakage across folds, but it cannot undo earlier cohort-wide exploration. This distinction is often obscured when a dataset split is called ``test'' even after repeated model repair.

The source-specific results also argue against interpreting the pooled metric as a universal operating point. DeepDRiD contributes a small negative denominator (52 cases) with 51 correctly cleared, while DDR and DRTiD provide much larger denominators and lower specificities. A pooled value summarizes this particular composition; it does not guarantee equivalent behavior in a new clinic, camera, geography, or prevalence setting.

The later transfer experiment is a useful negative result. A candidate trained with patient groups excluded from training and threshold selection can add a few clearances on an exposed cohort, yet the simple OR policy creates two misses. In the original replay, 270 of 385 nonreferable cases are cleared and 115 are referred; the 463 gradable referable and 133 ungradable records are referred. Small specificity gains are not acceptable when they break the safety constraint. A clinically meaningful next step is not another search over this reference pool; it is to freeze one complete image-to-action artifact and evaluate once on an untouched patient-level external cohort.

The frozen MMRDR-UWF test adds a stronger locked evaluation than the exposed development studies, but it remains a test partition within one dataset. The validation-selected operating point did not preserve zero observed misses, and specificity was only 20.02\%. Because patient identifiers are absent from the export, image-level intervals cannot establish patient-level uncertainty. The separate MMRDR validation comparisons and multi-source fine-tuning results remain development diagnostics; the latter's validation manifest included every historical reference eye. These findings do not show that a particular architecture or dataset is generally ineffective. They show that higher AUROC, a zero-miss validation cutoff, or more training data cannot substitute for patient-identifiable, independent confirmatory evaluation.

The referral-burden concern is not unique to this pipeline: on EyePACS, Jena and Rayguru report 17.5\% specificity for their best single model at 95\% sensitivity and lower unnecessary-referral burden for a weighted ensemble evaluated at a 90\% target \cite{jena2026}. This is contextual evidence that operating-point policy matters, not a performance comparison or external confirmation of ActiveRetina. Cohorts, labels, models, sensitivity targets, and evaluation procedures differ, so the numerical values must not be ranked across studies.

The candidate-panel result sharpens the selection-history concern, but its initial single-seed effect is not stable: four alternate paired partitions reverse its direction. A 2026 factorial radiomics study already decomposes cumulative analytical leakage through simulations and a TCIA cohort \cite{garciahidalgo2026}; we make no first-in-field claim for leakage auditing. Our narrower empirical question is the interaction of candidate-panel construction and patient-fold realization for a zero-miss retinal operating point. Nested cross-validation cannot erase the project's prior cohort exposure, and the result needs replication on a cohort and candidate library that did not influence development.

\subsection{What this study does and does not establish}
It establishes deterministic replay of a source-conditional score policy, reports patient-grouped development procedures, and evaluates a frozen ResNet-50 classifier once on an official within-dataset test partition. It does not establish calibrated probabilities, transportable zero-miss behavior, patient-level test uncertainty, independent-site generalization, clinical utility, prospective workflow safety, or clinical validation. The word ``coverage'' is used only for computational action assignment when specified; it must not be confused with the fraction of cases autonomously diagnosed by a validated system.

\section{Limitations}
The MMRDR six-logit referable score includes index 5 although the experiment has no grade-5 categorical training examples. Binary referable, pairwise-ranking, and hard-boundary losses may indirectly supervise this logit through their aggregate score, but it receives no direct grade-5 categorical supervision. Since the checkpoint is unavailable, no ablation can establish whether this design choice materially affected performance; the direction and magnitude are unknown.
First, the headline score-policy replay was built and tuned on the same 981-case cohort used for replay, and its component scores and local policy are not in the public checkout. Second, the frozen MMRDR-UWF evaluation is within one dataset, its released rows lack patient IDs, and its checkpoint plus full training configuration are not in the public checkout; it has five false-negative clearances and low specificity. A post-hoc SHA-256 audit found two test images duplicated byte-for-byte in model-fit data. Excluding them left the observed misses and specificity unchanged, but the official partition is therefore not wholly image-disjoint and the filtered result is not an independent confirmation. Exact-byte matching cannot detect recompressed or transformed near-duplicates or other patient overlap. Third, the 84.16\% multi-source accuracy result cross-fits only the threshold; its epoch was selected using the full validation labels, and the result has 113 missed referable images. Fourth, the ConvNeXt-Tiny run is an exposed raw-image validation experiment whose test was skipped and whose checkpoint/predictions are not bundled. Fifth, label definitions and acquisition differ across datasets, and pooled results are not population-standardized. Sixth, patient-grouped folds only protect their own fitting operations; prior project-wide inspection remains. No clinical trial, prospective deployment, independent-site validation, or patient-level risk certificate is included.

\section{Reproducibility and Data Availability}
The arXiv source archive contains this manuscript, aggregate supplements, and analysis code for selected audits. In particular, it includes an identifier-free receipt for the paired replay-versus-patient-grouped analysis: on the same exposed 981-eye cohort, eye-level specificity was 70.13\% (270/385) for deterministic replay and 69.61\% (268/385) for grouped calibration. Among 596 referable-or-ungradable eye records, replay had zero missed required eyes and grouped calibration had three (0/596 versus 3/596); the paired eye-level missed-required-eye-rate difference was +0.50 percentage points (conditional interval 0.00--1.17). Conditional source-stratified patient-cluster intervals were $[-1.31,0.00]$ percentage points for the specificity difference and $[0.00,1.17]$ points for the missed-required-eye-rate difference. These intervals use patient clusters as the resampling unit but retain eye-level endpoints; they quantify resampling variability conditional on observed decisions. They do not adjust for prior cohort-wide search, calibration or threshold selection, and are not independent validation. The archive does not contain the score-policy file, model checkpoints, prediction exports, or a complete executable reproduction bundle. The replay and paired audit require local row-level score/label inputs and a patient manifest that are not included; therefore neither result can be independently rerun from this archive. The aggregate supplements contain no dataset images, patient identifiers, per-eye labels, or per-eye scores. The MMRDR-UWF test result was computed from a local checkpoint and test export. Neither is included, and the checkpoint does not preserve the complete training invocation or feature-cache provenance. \section{Conclusion}
The frozen MMRDR-UWF ResNet-50 classifier achieved 99.61\% sensitivity and 20.02\% specificity on the source-designated image-level test split, with five false-negative clearances; its validation-selected zero-miss operating point did not transfer. A post-hoc exact-byte audit found two positive test images with copies in model-fit data. Their exclusion leaves the five misses and 20.02\% specificity unchanged, but confirms that the source-designated test partition is not wholly image-disjoint; this is not independent external validation. A distinct three-source ResNet-50 diagnostic produced 84.16\% threshold-crossfit accuracy but missed 113 referable images, and checkpoint selection had used the full validation set; this is post-selection evidence, not independent confirmation. The MMRDR export lacks patient IDs. Separately, the 70.13\% result is a score-level replay on its exposed 981-eye cohort, not a future-patient estimate. In a paired comparison on that same cohort, patient-grouped calibration retained similar eye-level specificity (69.61\% versus 70.13\%) but changed three referable or ungradable eye decisions from REFER to CLEAR. Among 596 referable-or-ungradable eye records, there were 3/596 versus 0/596 missed required eyes; the paired eye-level rate difference was +0.50 percentage points (conditional patient-cluster bootstrap 95\% interval, 0.00--1.17). Patient clusters are the resampling unit, not the endpoint. This conditional analysis does not adjust for prior exploration or establish future-patient risk; the interval includes zero. In the nested panel analysis, the retrospectively highest result with fewer than five misses is 60.78\% specificity with three misses, selected across ten repeated panel-by-seed outcomes. Across five paired seeds the wider panel averaged 1.14 percentage points lower specificity, the difference changed sign once, and neither panel achieved zero misses. These findings support a bounded evidence audit of operating-point transfer and panel-by-split instability, not a novel general-purpose estimator, autonomous model, or clinical validation. Independent, patient-identifiable replication is required before broader claims.
